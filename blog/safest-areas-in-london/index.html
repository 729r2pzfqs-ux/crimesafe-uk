Safest Areas in London 2026 — Borough Rankings

London has 32 boroughs and the City of London, each with very different crime profiles. We've ranked them all using official Metropolitan Police data so you can find the safest places to live, work, and raise a family.

Key Finding: The safest London boroughs are concentrated in outer London, particularly the south-west. Richmond upon Thames tops the 2026 rankings with a safety score of 8.9/10, recording just 58 crimes per 1,000 residents. That's less than half the rate of central London boroughs like Westminster.

How We Rank London Boroughs

Our safety scores are built from official police-recorded crime data published by the Metropolitan Police Service and the City of London Police. We calculate a composite score (out of 10) that accounts for:

  • Total crime rate — crimes per 1,000 residents over the past 12 months
  • Violent crime weighting — violence against the person is weighted more heavily than low-harm offences
  • Trend direction — boroughs where crime is falling get a slight boost; rising crime counts against them
  • Anti-social behaviour — ASB incidents are included as they affect quality of life

The result is a single number that makes it straightforward to compare boroughs at a glance. You can explore full neighbourhood-level breakdowns on our London crime page.

However, Westminster's figures are inflated by its daytime population of workers and tourists, who far outnumber actual residents.

What Makes a Borough Safe?

Several factors contribute to lower crime rates in outer London boroughs:

Community and Demographics

Boroughs with high home ownership rates, established families, and strong community organisations tend to experience less crime. Areas like Harrow and Havering have active neighbourhood watch schemes and close-knit communities.

Lower Footfall

Central London boroughs deal with millions of visitors daily, creating more opportunities for theft and anti-social behaviour. Outer boroughs have far fewer transient visitors, which naturally reduces opportunistic crime.

Policing and Resources

While the Metropolitan Police allocates resources based on need, safer boroughs often benefit from effective Safer Neighbourhood Teams and community policing approaches that address issues before they escalate.

Boroughs to Watch in 2026

Several boroughs are showing improving trends that could see them climb the rankings:

  • Barnet — crime down 3.2% year-on-year, currently just outside the top 10 with a safety score of 7.6
  • Enfield — new investment in CCTV and community policing is showing results, with a 5% reduction in residential burglary
  • Croydon — after years of higher crime, the borough has made significant strides, with violent crime down 7% since 2024

Tips for Choosing a Safe Area

If you're planning to move to London or relocate within the city, here's how to assess safety:

  1. Check the postcode — use our postcode checker to see reported crimes within a one-mile radius of any address.
  2. Visit at different times — an area can feel different during the day versus at night, or on weekdays versus weekends.
  3. Look beyond the headline numbers — a borough with high overall crime might have very safe residential pockets. Drill down to street-level data.
  4. Consider crime types — some areas have higher shoplifting (around high streets) but very low violent crime. Think about what matters most to you.
  5. Check trends, not just snapshots — a borough with improving numbers may be a better bet than one that's been safe but is seeing rises.
  6. Talk to locals — online forums for specific neighbourhoods can give you insight that raw data misses.
